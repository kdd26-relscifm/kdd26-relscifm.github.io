Welcome

About RelSciFM

RelSciFM focuses on building reliable Scientific Foundation Models (SciFMs) for scientific discovery and high-stakes decision-making. The workshop emphasizes design and training, grounding to scientific evidence, and verification protocols that make model claims auditable and replayable.

We bring together researchers and practitioners across data mining, machine learning, and domain sciences to share methods, datasets, benchmarks, and best practices for reliable, grounded, and verifiable SciFMs.

Overview

Topics

Key themes and questions we explore at RelSciFM.

Architecture Design with Scientific Priors

Inductive biases for invariances, conservation laws, units, and geometry

Evidence Grounding and Provenance

Retrieval over databases and knowledge graphs with explicit attribution

Tool- and Simulator-Integrated Modeling

Reliable interfaces to simulators, solvers, and symbolic engines

Reliable Data and Pretraining

Curation for quality and coverage; robust objectives under shift

Uncertainty and Risk Control

Calibration, abstention/defer, and risk–coverage trade-offs

Validity, Robustness, and Stress Tests

Constraint checks, invariance tests, closed-loop tool validation

Reproducibility and Auditing

Versioned pipelines, seed capture, and tool-trace logs

Benchmarks and Community Testbeds

Reliability-focused benchmarks and shared evaluation harnesses

Panel Discussion
Question 1 / 6
Q1
When you say that a scientific foundation model is reliable, what is the minimum evidence you would require before allowing it to influence a real scientific decision?
Could you give one concrete example from your own domain?
Panel Discussion
Question 2 / 6
Q2
Which failure mode is more dangerous in scientific AI: a confidently wrong answer, a correct answer reached for the wrong reason, or a model that abstains too often?
Panel Discussion
Question 3 / 6
Q3
Should the community aim for one broadly general scientific foundation model, or a family of domain-specialized models?
What, if anything, can reliably transfer across scientific domains?
Panel Discussion
Question 4 / 6
Q4
Where should scientific knowledge enter a foundation model most effectively: in the architecture, the training objective, external tools and simulators, or the verification stage?
Panel Discussion
Question 5 / 6
Q5
What is one result that may look impressive on an academic benchmark but would still not convince you to deploy the model in a real scientific workflow?
Panel Discussion
Question 6 / 6
Q6
How much autonomy should we give scientific agents today?
Which parts of the scientific workflow can be automated safely, and which parts should still require explicit human approval?
